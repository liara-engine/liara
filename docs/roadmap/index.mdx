## How to read this

Every milestone has a scope, a list of what is explicitly not in scope, and a Definition of Done. The out-of-scope list matters as much as the scope: it is what keeps a version from growing while it is being built.

Precision decreases in three steps. **Phase 0** is specified down to the file. **v0.1 through v1.0** are specified at the feature level. **Post-v1.0** is specified only as themes, and will be scoped when v1.0 ships, with the benefit of having actually used the engine.

That stratification is an honest assessment of what can be planned and what cannot. A solo project's priorities six months out are shaped mostly by what the first six months teach, and pretending otherwise produces detailed roadmaps that get shredded on contact with reality.

## Cadence

There is no calendar. No "v0.3 in March". A version ships when its Definition of Done is met, CI is green and the documentation is updated.

That acknowledges how this project actually gets built: a hyperfocus period may produce two releases in a week, and academic or external commitments may produce a month of silence. Both are normal, and the roadmap neither stresses about the silence nor slows down the bursts.

Four things follow.

**Scope is bounded per version.** A milestone is sized to finish inside one focus burst. A version that starts to feel like it will not is too big, and gets split. Open-ended versions invite abandonment.

**Pauses are not failures.** The roadmap grants explicit permission to not touch the project for weeks. The next burst picks up where the last one stopped, with the milestone page as the bookmark, and there is no such thing as falling behind.

**Definitions of Done are checkable.** Every item is objectively verifiable: a test passes, a binary runs, a page exists. No item reads "feature X feels good".

**Developer tools ship with the features they observe.** A new subsystem without a way to inspect its state is incomplete, so the tools are part of each version's Definition of Done rather than a workstream that lags behind. All of them are compiled out of release builds or hidden behind a toggle that is off by default.

## Version numbers

**A milestone name is not a version number, and from v0.1 they deliberately differ.** "v0.4" names a milestone on this page. The meta repository shipping it is at 4.x.y. The shift is one level: a milestone's minor is a repository's major.

**Everything at 0.x belongs to Phase 0.** From v0.1 onward, a repository's first breaking change takes it to 1.0.0, and each breaking change after that increments the major normally. The meta repository therefore tracks the milestones exactly: Phase 0 was 0.x, v0.1 is 1.x, v0.2 is 2.x. Which module versions go with a given meta release is recorded in the manifests, as it always was.

**Two digits were the reason.** Staying on 0.x until the project's own v1.0 was the original plan, and it left only the minor and the patch to express everything that happens to a contract that breaks on purpose. That is one field too few.

**The ABI tends to follow the same rhythm**, because a milestone needing a breaking change to the contract is what moves both. ABI 4.x.y beside milestone v0.4 reads well, and keeping that correspondence where it falls out naturally is worth a little care. It is not worth twisting a release for: a milestone that needs no breaking change to the contract leaves the ABI where it is, and the correspondence slips. That is the right outcome, since a version describes what happened to the contract rather than which milestone was open at the time.

**Modules drift more, and that is expected.** They break less often than the contract does, so their majors fall behind. A module's major says what happened to that module and nothing else. One manual intervention is anticipated: a module still at 0.x when the project reaches v1.0 will probably be moved to 1.0.0 by hand, because 0.x advertises an instability a shipped engine contradicts — unless the module is young enough that 0.x is still telling the truth.

**`docs-shared` and `.github` follow none of this.** They version on their own cycles, dissociated from the milestones entirely: infrastructure consumed by CI and the documentation pipeline, with no ABI and no milestone. `.github` sitting at 2.x while the engine is at v0.1 means only that it has had two breaking changes of its own.

How release-please is configured to produce all of that is in [Commits and releases](../tooling/commits-and-releases/#release-please).

## This page changes

Shipped versions are annotated as shipped. Unshipped ones may be revised in light of experience, under three rules.

A version's scope may shrink, and features may move to a later version. It should not silently grow, because adding scope to a version in flight is how vaporware starts. A Definition of Done may become more precise, and an item is not removed without a reason written down. The post-v1.0 themes are freely revisable, which is what they are for.

The review happens at the end of every shipped version, and it either confirms the next one's plan or changes it.
